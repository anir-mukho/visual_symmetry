What do these four have in common?

Brickwork

slidesone brick along a course; and half a brick across, one course up
turnsa half turn about the centre of every brick
quarter turnsnone — a brick is not square
mirrorsdown the middle of every brick, down every upright joint, and along the middle of every course
glidesreflect in the line between two courses, then slide half a brick along
G = ⟨ u, v, h, m ⟩ u, v the two slides  ·  h a half turn  ·  m a mirror

A tiled floor

slidesone tile across, one tile up
turnsa quarter turn about every tile centre and every tile corner; a half turn about every edge midpoint
mirrorsfour directions through each quarter-turn centre — upright, flat, and both diagonals
glidesdiagonal lines, halfway between the diagonal mirrors
G = ⟨ u, v, q, m ⟩ u, v the two slides  ·  q a quarter turn  ·  m a mirror

Woven cloth

slidesdiagonally, from one crossing to the next, in two directions
turnsa quarter turn about the centre of every square gap; a half turn at every crossing
mirrorsalong the centre line of every strand, in two directions — but not one of them passes through a quarter-turn centre
glideslines running midway between the strands
G = ⟨ u, v, q, m ⟩ the same four kinds of move as the floor — arranged differently, and not the same group

A star tiling

slidesone cell across, one cell up
turnsa quarter turn about every star centre and every cross centre; a half turn between them
mirrorsfour directions through each quarter-turn centre — upright, flat, and both diagonals
glidesdiagonal lines, halfway between the diagonal mirrors
G = ⟨ u, v, q, m ⟩ word for word the tiled floor’s list — the same group, on a pattern that looks nothing like it
