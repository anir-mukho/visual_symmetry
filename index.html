A first course in group theory

Four lectures on symmetry, for undergraduates meeting groups for the first time. Everything below runs in the browser — nothing to install, and once a page has loaded it needs no connection.

Seventeen Patterns

opening slides

Brickwork, a tiled floor, woven cloth and a star tiling. Two of them have exactly the same symmetry despite looking nothing alike, and two look alike but do not. Arrow keys to advance, F for full screen.

Reading a Wall in the Alhambra

worked example

A photograph of fourteenth-century tilework, and the symmetry group worked out from it in three steps: find the lattice, find the highest rotation, look for a mirror. The answer is p3 — and the six-pointed stars are not six-fold centres.

One demo per lecture

Symmetry Lab

lecture 1

Turn and flip a polygon and watch where the corners go. Compare rf with fr side by side, and build the Cayley table by exploring.

Isomorphism Detective

lecture 2

Two Cayley tables, coloured so they match exactly when your dictionary is an isomorphism. Hunt for one — or read off the invariant that proves none exists.

Permutation Workshop

lecture 3

Shuffles drawn as strands, where the crossings are what you count. Then the 15-puzzle, with the invariant that makes Sam Loyd's position impossible sitting in plain sight.

Cosets and Orbits

lecture 4

A subgroup as one cluster and its cosets as identical copies, so Lagrange's theorem becomes counting. Then a cube you can turn, giving 6×4 = 8×3 = 12×2 = 24.
