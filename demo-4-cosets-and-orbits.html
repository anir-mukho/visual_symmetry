Cosets and Orbits

A subgroup sits inside a group as one cluster, and its cosets are perfect copies of that cluster. Once you see the copies, Lagrange’s theorem is just counting them. Then the same theorem comes back as orbit times stabiliser.

A subgroup and its copies

Pick a group and a subgroup. The graph has one node per element and one edge per generator, and the subgroup’s own edges cut it into clusters. Count the clusters, count a cluster, multiply.

Orbit × stabiliser

Choose a shape, choose what the symmetries are allowed to move, then click one of those things. Everything it can be sent to is its orbit; everything that leaves it alone is its stabiliser. The two multiply to the size of the whole group, every time, because the orbit is in bijection with the cosets of the stabiliser.
